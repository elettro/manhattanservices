Dog walking services in Manhattan.

Choose a reliable walking routine for workdays, long schedules, puppies, senior dogs, and recurring apartment visits.

Published by Elettro IncorporatedReviewed August 19, 2026

How do you choose a dog walker in Manhattan?

Start with a meet-and-greet. Confirm experience with your dog’s size, age, behavior, equipment, and neighborhood. Define walk length, solo or group service, access security, update requirements, emergency procedures, insurance, and backup coverage in writing.

Define the walking routine

State the preferred time window, walk length, pace, route restrictions, elevator or stair process, feeding, water, waste disposal, and weather limits. Explain pulling, reactivity, escape risk, anxiety, medical conditions, and interactions to avoid.

Ask whether walks are solo or grouped. If grouped, confirm the number of dogs, matching process, transportation, and how the walker handles conflicts.

Screen the person entering your home

  • Ask about references, background screening, insurance, and training.
  • Document keys, access codes, doorman procedures, and authorized walkers.
  • Confirm photo, GPS, or written visit updates.
  • Ask how emergencies, lost keys, injuries, delays, and severe weather are handled.
  • Understand who covers the route when the regular walker is unavailable.

Prepare your dog and apartment

Keep the leash, harness, bags, towel, medication instructions, and treats in one labeled place. Update tags and microchip information. Leave veterinary and emergency contacts where the walker can reach them quickly.

Tell building staff who is authorized to enter. If equipment fit, behavior, or health changes, update the walker before the next visit.

Compare the full service, not one rate

Walking prices vary with visit length, frequency, solo or group format, number of dogs, timing, holidays, special handling, and added feeding or medication tasks. Ask whether the rate covers entry time, towel drying, water, feeding, visit updates, cancellations, and last-minute requests. A consistent provider with secure access and reliable backup coverage often delivers more value than the lowest advertised rate.

Dog-walking FAQ

What should a visit include?

Visit length, activity, feeding or water tasks, equipment, waste handling, updates, and a plan if the dog cannot be walked.

Should a walker have backup coverage?

Recurring clients should ask what happens when the primary walker is sick, delayed, or unavailable.

How should keys be handled?

Use a documented access process, secure storage, identification rules, a return procedure, and a list of authorized walkers.
